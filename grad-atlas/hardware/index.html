From maths to hardware.

Choose the work before choosing the degree.

Chip design, wafer manufacturing and embedded systems lead to different jobs and need different preparation. These routes are ranked by admissions evidence and technical training, not promised employment.

Four different kinds of hardware work

Design the chip

Digital logic, RTL, verification, architecture and accelerators. Maths helps with formal verification and numerical datapaths.

Make the wafer

Devices, process integration, metrology and yield. Statistics complements semiconductor physics; it does not replace it.

Package & test

Reliability, thermal and mechanical constraints, integration and production test. Electronics meets materials and measurement.

Build the system

FPGA and microcontrollers, firmware, interfaces and board debugging. Real hardware work, distinct from wafer fabrication.

Where to start looking

  1. Fabrication-facing training: NUS Semiconductor Technology and Operations explicitly names mathematics. Choose technical courses and inspect the internship project scope.
  2. Accelerators, FPGA and embedded: NUS Computer Engineering explicitly names applied mathematics. A general or pure-maths degree still needs an individual assessment.
  3. A foundation in engineering: BU LEAP is an explicit non-engineering conversion route, with extra foundation time and cost.
  4. Hong Kong options: CityU manufacturing and EEE are conditional candidates. Broad science/degree wording is not confirmed maths eligibility or remedial circuits training.

Purdue online explicitly includes mathematics, but is a skills route rather than a campus or relocation substitute. All routes retain their caveats below.

Turn a maths transcript into engineering evidence

  • Digital: logic → C/C++ → Verilog/SystemVerilog → architecture → an FPGA project with tests and timing reports.
  • Manufacturing: circuits and semiconductor physics → process flow → statistical process control → reproducible yield or metrology analysis.
  • Packaging/test: electronics, heat transfer/materials, reliability statistics and instrumented test automation.

These preparation suggestions are advisory, not additional university admission rules.
